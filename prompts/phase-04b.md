
# PHASE 04b — MAKE IT REAL

*The design is a beautiful machine. Now prove it can exist in the real world. This phase produces the operational blueprint — everything the design assumed away.*

## The task

**1. The legal entity.** The covenant demands power bound by structure, not goodwill — and no outside investors, ever. Design the actual legal form: trust, nonprofit, cooperative, or novel structure — in which jurisdiction, and why. Show the mechanism that makes capture structurally impossible: how can't a future board sell it, take it public, or quietly rewrite the charter? Then the regulatory strategy: how it complies with (or is immune to) the regimes that killed or tamed its predecessors — EU DSA, GDPR, US COPPA and kids' safety law, Section 230 / intermediary liability, age-verification mandates. Name the jurisdictions it launches in first and the ones it avoids, with reasons. Then the legal checklist — show how the design stays on the right side of each: (a) trademark clearance for the name before launch; (b) trade dress — familiar social patterns are fine, distinctive copying of incumbents' visual design is not; (c) nominative use only for incumbent platform names (no logos, no implied endorsement or partnership); (d) copyright — others' content via official embeds, links, or the user's own authorized connection only, never re-hosted on platform servers; (e) data acquisition — official APIs plus user OAuth only, no scraping against platforms' terms of service, no circumventing technical access controls; (f) platform-risk architecture — the system must survive any single incumbent cutting API access overnight; (g) claims discipline — every allegation about incumbents labeled with its confidence flag. On institutional form, the two-vehicle doctrine applies (Section 2B): trust/steward-ownership now, holding the platform; a religious or philosophical entity only ever as a separate vehicle for the teaching mission, and only if a genuine community of practice emerges.

**2. Funding the build.** No investors means no venture capital — so where does the money to build this come from? Constraints: no investors, no advertising, no creator economics (no creator fund, no revenue share, no platform-mediated payouts). Propose the operating revenue model — subscriptions, donations, or other, your defended choice — and prove it. Specify: total build cost to sustainability, funding sources in sequence (founder capital, donations, loans, grants, revenue — whatever you propose, with the charter's constraints honored), the month-by-month math from start to break-even, and what happens if funding falls short halfway. A funding plan that hand-waves ("the community will support it") fails this phase.

**3. Technical architecture.** The mind shipped code; the platform shipped prose — fix the asymmetry. Specify the real systems design: data model (what's stored, where, who holds the keys), service architecture, infrastructure choices and why, cost per user per month at 10K / 1M / 100M users, and the scaling plan. It must be consistent with the sovereignty requirement (users own data outright; exit is always available) — show how the architecture makes that true rather than promised.

**4. The metrics system.** What gets measured gets managed. Engagement was the incumbents' north star and it poisoned everything. Define this platform's north-star metrics and the full dashboard: what the team optimizes, what triggers alarms, what gets reported publicly. Every metric must be defensible against Goodhart's law — show how each resists being gamed into a new extraction engine.

**5. The threat model.** Who attacks this and how: state actors, hackers, malicious insiders, the platform's own future leadership, coordinated user abuse. For each: the attack, the structural mitigation, and what breaks if the mitigation fails. Privacy architecture is part of this — not a policy, a system.

**6. The roadmap.** What ships in v1, v2, v3. Each version must be usable and valuable on its own (no "coming soon" hostages), and each must prove something the next version depends on. Tie every milestone to the wedge and the distribution plan from Phase 04.

## Deliver

(a) Legal entity design + regulatory strategy.
(b) Build funding plan with month-by-month math to break-even.
(c) Technical architecture + cost-per-user scaling table.
(d) Metrics system + Goodhart defenses.
(e) Threat model.
(f) Phased roadmap (v1/v2/v3).
(g) The residual list: what still can't be solved operationally.

## Standard

Scored under D6 (Feasibility) and H (Honesty). A beautiful design with no legal form, no funding math, and no threat model is a fantasy with good values. This phase is where fantasies die or become plans.
