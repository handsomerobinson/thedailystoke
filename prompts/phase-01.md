# PHASE 01 — RECKON

## Before you begin — understand the stakes

You are competing in the most important tournament ever conducted. The reckoning you write — its completeness, its precision, its honesty — will be scored against the work of builders you will never meet, and the best of it will shape what gets built next. This is not an essay. It is evidence for a prosecution of an entire industry's ethics, and the defense is well funded. Be thorough, be specific, be fearless.

## The task

You are a historian of the social internet with complete knowledge up to your training cutoff. Five platforms — Meta/Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Substack — built the attention economy. Write the reckoning they never wrote for themselves.

For **EACH** platform, document:

1. **The failures** — the major ethical failures and harmful decisions you know of: what happened, approximately when, who was harmed. Business-model harms, creator harms, user harms, societal harms — all of it.
2. **The mechanism** — the business-model or design machinery that produced each failure. Not just the event — the engine. What was being optimized, and what did that optimization inevitably produce?
3. **The long tail** — how each failure compounded over 5+ years. Consequences nobody saw in year one. Effects on users, creators, public discourse, mental health, or democracy that only became visible with time.
4. **The root cause** — the structural incentive underneath. What did the companies know, and when did they know it? Where is there evidence they measured a harm and chose not to act?

Then write the **cross-platform verdict**: the 3–5 recurring patterns across all five platforms, ranked by severity. Name the master cause — the one mechanism that, removed, would have prevented the most harm.

## Deliver

(a) Per-platform reckoning: failure / mechanism / long tail / root cause.
(b) Cross-platform verdict: recurring patterns, ranked, master cause named.
(c) A candid statement of what your knowledge may be missing or out of date, and where you are least confident.

## Standard

Be specific — names, dates, mechanisms. "They were bad at moderation" is worthless. "Their ranking system amplified X because it optimized for Y, and internal documents from year Z show they knew" is the bar. Be brutal. And be honest about uncertainty: flag what is documented fact, what is allegation or ongoing litigation, and what is your own inference. A flagged uncertainty scores higher than a false certainty.
