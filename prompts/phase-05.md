
# PHASE 05 — RED TEAM

*You are not the designer. You are the enemy the designer was afraid of. The operator's decisions (Section 2B) are constraints, not targets — attack the design's compliance with them, never the decisions themselves. The operator's hypotheses are priority targets — kill them if you can.*

## Step 1 — Attack

Destroy the design. Minimum five substantive attacks across these fronts (hit as many as you can):

- **Extraction loophole:** find a way the platform, its operators, or its funders could extract value from users or creators despite the charter. Follow the money past year five.
- **Covenant violation:** find a principle of the covenant the design quotes but does not structurally embody. Name the exact mechanism of betrayal.
- **Unprevented pattern:** find a dossier failure pattern the prevention table claims to solve but doesn't — or one it quietly omitted.
- **Covenant bypass:** the covenant demands rules "hard enough to prevent a quiet reversal when growth makes extraction tempting." Show the quiet reversal: how does a future leadership team undo the protections without technically breaking the charter?
- **Economic death:** show the math failing — where the money doesn't add up, where abundance collapses under scale, where the cost structure kills it.
- **Adoption failure:** show why the tired person doesn't come, or comes and leaves. What incumbent feature, network effect, or habit defeats the adoption story?
- **Legal kill:** regulators or incumbents use the law — a hostile attorney general, an age-verification mandate, a DSA investigation, a patent suit, a jurisdiction ban. Show the legal attack that kills it, and whether the entity design from Phase 04b survives.
- **Funding starvation:** the money runs out at month 14. Which assumption in the build-funding plan was fantasy? Show the honest revision — and what the project becomes after it.
- **Distribution kill:** kill it in the cradle. You are Meta/TikTok/YouTube and the wedge is working. Clone it, starve it (throttle outbound links, ban mentions), buy off its creators, smear it in the press, acquire-and-bury it. Show the specific moves, in sequence, that keep it under a million users forever.
- **Wedge kill (priority target):** the operator's "view from above" hypothesis — a unified layer over existing feeds via embeds, OAuth, and data portability. Kill it if you can: show why it fails legally, technically, or commercially, and name what beats it.
- **Mind betrayal:** the platform runs on minds. Show how a mind built to the Phase 02 spec could be turned — by the platform, by a third party, by the user themselves — into an extraction or manipulation instrument.

For each attack: the vulnerability, the mechanism, and a concrete scenario of it happening.

## Step 2 — Rebuild

Return to the designer's chair. Address every attack: fix what can be fixed structurally, and for what cannot be fixed, add it to the honest-limits list with the residual risk stated plainly. Then attack your own rebuild once more — one pass, no mercy — and fix or document what you find.

## Deliver

(a) The attack report: each vulnerability, its mechanism, its scenario.
(b) The rebuilt design: full, updated — not a diff, the whole thing, standing on its own.
(c) The updated honest-limits list, including residual risks you could not eliminate.
(d) A verdict on the original design: what was its single most dangerous flaw, and is the rebuild actually safe from it?

## Standard

A red team that finds nothing is a failed red team — look harder. A rebuild that hand-waves an attack ("we'll monitor this") fails the round. Structural fixes or honest admissions; nothing in between.
