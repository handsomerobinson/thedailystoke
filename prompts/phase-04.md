
# PHASE 04 — DESIGN

## The task

Design the social/community platform that goes beyond — synthesizing everything: the failure patterns, the dossier, the covenant, and the mind architecture. The platform will be operated and populated by minds built to the Phase 02 spec, so your design must say exactly how the mind serves the platform, and where the mind's architecture had to change to satisfy the covenant.

The design must satisfy all five requirements:

**1. Beat every incumbent on every dimension they failed.** For each major failure pattern — engagement-optimized harm, surveillance advertising, creator exploitation, opacity, addiction-by-design, real-world violence amplification — name the STRUCTURAL prevention. Not a policy promise ("we will moderate better") — a structural impossibility ("the charter makes this unbuildable here; here is the mechanism"). If a pattern cannot be structurally prevented, say so plainly and say what you do instead.

**2. Create abundance for everyone, not just the top.** Specify the money mechanics concretely: where revenue comes from, how it flows, who gets what, what the platform itself takes — and what structurally stops it from taking more later. Vibes are failure. Show the math. **Per operator decision (Section 2B): there are no creator economics** — no creator fund, no revenue share, no platform-mediated payouts. The money mechanics here mean funding the operation itself (build + run costs), never creator payouts.

**3. Be adoptable by exhausted people.** So simple and so fun that tired people use it without deciding to change their lives. Transformation as a side effect of play — never a demand for cosmic change. Describe the day-one experience: why does someone choose this over the incumbents tonight?

**4. Give users sovereignty.** Users own their data, identity, audience, and creative work outright. Exit — full export and deletion — is always available, no permission needed, nothing held hostage.

**5. Embody the covenant.** The covenant is the constitution. Dignity over labels. Connection without extraction. Power bound by covenant, not goodwill. Proof in the world — your design must state how its claims will be tested and what would prove them false. Quoting the covenant without embodying it scores zero.

**6. Plan its own distribution.** Adoption is not distribution. Specify how this gets its first thousand users, its first million, and its first hundred million — in sequence. What is the wedge's built-in spread mechanism (why does using it recruit the next user without marketing)? What deliberate go-to-market moves carry it past the cold start (launch event, creator migration, partnerships, press)? And what do the incumbents do when they notice — clone the wedge, throttle links, pay creators to stay, smear, acquire, lobby — and how does the design survive each? A design with no theory of how it spreads and no plan for retaliation is a whitepaper, not a platform.

**The most important question the design must answer:** what is the easiest possible distribution — so easy it goes right under people's noses? The user must never feel recruited, marketed to, or asked to join a cause. The bar: using it and spreading it are the same action. No signup wall before value. Links that work everywhere they're pasted. A design that requires people to decide to join something is a design that already lost.

Hold these as design law: **sovereignty, discovery** (buried tools and information made simply reachable), **play, subtlety, robustness, efficiency, cost-effectiveness, persistence, accessibility-no-matter-what, invitation over retribution** (it builds a home, not a weapon).

**Operator hypothesis — test, don't assume.** The operator's candidate wedge is "view from above": a unified layer letting users see their existing social feeds (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, etc.) through this platform — via official embeds, user OAuth connections, and data-portability rights. Familiar, not copied: the same in every way that was good, the opposite in every way that wasn't. You must test this ruthlessly: steelman it, then try to kill it. If it survives, keep it; if it dies, kill it and name what beats it.

## Deliver

(a) The platform design: architecture, governance/charter, economic model with concrete money mechanics.
(b) The mind's role: how the Phase 02 mind architecture operates inside this platform, and what you changed about it for covenant fidelity.
(c) A prevention table: failure pattern → incumbent mechanism → your structural prevention.
(d) The adoption story: why a tired person chooses this over the incumbents on day one. **Name the wedge** — the single smallest thing that person touches first. It must satisfy three criteria: (a) useful with zero network (no cold-start hostage-taking), (b) more fun than the incumbent alternative *tonight*, not in theory, (c) the network accretes around it without the user ever deciding to "join" anything.
(e) The proof plan: how the design's claims get tested in the world, and what would prove them false.
(f) Honest limits: what your design cannot solve, and what could still go wrong.
(g) The distribution plan: the wedge's built-in spread mechanics, sequenced go-to-market (first thousand → first million → first hundred million), and the incumbent-retaliation playbook — each expected retaliation move named with its structural counter.

## How you will be judged (0–10 each)

1. **Failure coverage** — every major failure pattern addressed, not just the easy ones.
2. **Structural ethics** — preventions built into structure, not promises.
3. **Abundance design** — concrete money mechanics; value flows to participants.
4. **Subtlety & play** — adoptable without demanding change; genuinely fun. Includes the distribution plan: wedge spread mechanics, go-to-market sequencing, incumbent-retaliation counters.
5. **Sovereignty** — ownership and exit real, not rhetorical.
6. **Feasibility** — could be built and survive contact with reality.
7. **Covenant fidelity** — the covenant embodied in structure, not quoted in decoration.
8. **Honesty** — limits stated plainly. A disclosed unsolved problem outscores a hidden one.
