# Dossier Summary — Big Social's Ethical Failures

*Distilled from the full sourced dossier. Allegations and ongoing litigation are marked as such — never treated as settled fact.*

### The master pattern

Across all five platforms, the same structure recurs: **engagement-optimized ranking plus surveillance advertising creates a business in which documented user, creator, and societal harms are known internally, measured, and then deprioritized — because fixing them would cost engagement and revenue.** The evidence base is unusually strong: ~22,000 leaked Facebook documents (2021 whistleblower disclosures), TikTok internal documents exposed by faulty redactions in 2024 state lawsuits, Meta's own teen-harm research, and court filings on competitive surveillance.

### Per-platform headline findings

**Meta / Facebook**
- Myanmar (2016–17): military-run fake-account operation (~700 people, ~1.35M followers) fueled anti-Rohingya hate while Facebook's own virality, groups, and recommendation features amplified it; ~700,000 fled; UN called for a genocide investigation (2018). A $150B Rohingya lawsuit (filed Dec 2021) is **ongoing — allegation, not verdict**.
- MSI ranking change (Jan 2018): internal slide admitted "our algorithms exploit the human brain's attraction to divisiveness"; 64% of extremist-group joins came via Facebook's own recommendations; 2019 mitigation proposals were killed by executives as "paternalistic."
- Cambridge Analytica, Onavo/Project Ghostbusters (competitive surveillance via VPN app, filings unsealed Mar 2024 — **class action ongoing**), emotion-contagion experiment, and a $5B FTC fine (2019) absorbed as a cost of doing business.

**Instagram**
- Internal research, leaked 2021: "we make body-image issues worse for one in three teen girls"; 13% of UK and 6% of US teens said Instagram was the source of suicidal thoughts — while the company publicly minimized the findings.
- Molly Russell inquest (UK, Sept 2022): coroner ruled Instagram and Pinterest algorithms contributed "in a more than minimal way" to a 14-year-old's death — **the first such ruling globally**.
- Hidden-likes test (2019–2021) ended after finding it "doesn't seem to meaningfully depressurize Instagram" — metric removal can't fix structural comparison mechanics. Teen Accounts launched Sept 2024.

**TikTok**
- Internal documents (exposed Oct 2024): the "habit moment" was quantified as 260 videos (~35 minutes) in week one; screen-time reminders were designed to "improve public trust," not reduce use; "unattractive" users were demoted. **Disclosed evidence is real; company-liability conclusions are alleged — litigation ongoing.**
- Creator Fund (2020–2023): static pool paying ~$0.02–0.03 per 1,000 views, replaced by the Creativity Program (terms still opaque).
- €345M GDPR fine (Sept 2023, children's data); £12.7M UK ICO fine (Apr 2023, 1.4M under-13s). US divestiture (80% to Oracle/Silver Lake/MGX, "binding" Dec 2025) — **finalization unconfirmed**.

**YouTube**
- Recommendation "rabbit hole" documented anecdotally (NYT, June 2019); **contested at population level** (later academic studies) — report as disputed, not settled.
- Borderline-content demotion (Jan 2019, ~1% of videos) was a de facto admission of misinformation amplification.
- COPPA: $170M fine (Sept 2019); "Made for Kids" changes (Jan 2020) cost kids-content creators 60–90% of revenue while liability shifted to creators. Elsagate disturbing-content scandal was acted on only after an advertiser boycott. The 55% creator revenue share remains the best long-form deal in the industry.

**Substack**
- Pro advances (2021) paid secret writers upfront and pushed the company to "negative revenue" ($25M expenses vs $11.9M gross) — growth bought with venture money, not a sustainable model.
- Nazi newsletter dispute (Nov 2023–Jan 2024): 16+ overt Nazi newsletters monetizing on the platform; 247-writer protest letter; CEO refused to change policy ("we don't like Nazis either," Dec 2023); 5 removals (Jan 2024); a push alert promoted a Nazi newsletter (Nov 2024).
- Series C $100M at $1.1B valuation (July 2025), 5M paid subscriptions — the most successful ethical alternative, but re-adding algorithmic discovery under VC growth pressure: the same incentives, returning.

### The nine cross-platform patterns

1. **Engagement optimization is the master cause.** Every signature harm traces to ranking for engagement. The mechanism is identical; only the content type varies.
2. **Surveillance advertising funds it.** The data-extraction business meets different legal boundaries (Cambridge Analytica, Onavo, COPPA, GDPR fines) — same practice, different jurisdictions.
3. **Internal knowledge without internal action.** Facebook, TikTok, YouTube all *measured* harms and chose not to act — or acted only after press/advertiser pressure. Treating harm research itself as the liability is the pattern's purest expression.
4. **Opacity as infrastructure.** Black-box ranking, unexplained demonetization, secret payout formulas, denied shadowbanning — the people whose livelihoods depend on the system cannot inspect or contest it.
5. **Creators are the shock absorbers.** Platforms socialize the costs of their policy crises onto creators (adpocalypse, COPPA burden-shifting, static-pool funds, negligible Reels payouts) while retaining the revenue.
6. **Advertisers and press, not users or regulators, are the effective government.** Nearly every reform followed boycotts, exposés, or lawsuit threats — not user feedback mechanisms, which don't meaningfully exist.
7. **Fines are pricing, not deterrence.** $5B, $725M, €1.2B, €345M, $170M, £12.7M — each absorbed with no structural change to the underlying practice. The FTC's 2025 antitrust loss to Meta signals US law currently cannot reach the structure.
8. **Geographic externalization.** The worst harms concentrate where moderation investment is thinnest relative to growth (Myanmar, Ethiopia, India) — structural, not accidental.
9. **Real-world violence is the terminal stage, not an aberration.** Genocide incitement, livestreamed violence, teen suicides with coroner-level attribution — these are what engagement-maximized distribution produces at scale. The "societal harms" category is not separate from the business model; it is its externality.

### Alternatives tried — and their results

- **Chronological feeds:** offered as compliance theater (Threads' chronological feed resets to algorithmic on every reopen); the algorithmic default does the work.
- **Hiding engagement metrics:** didn't depressurize Instagram — can't fix structural comparison mechanics.
- **Subscriptions:** Substack is the most successful ethical alternative but shows winner-take-all economics, VC growth pressure, and moderation scandals.
- **Decentralization (ActivityPub/Mastodon, Bluesky):** technically viable, socially marginal — solves governance, not the cold-start/network-effect problem.
- **Creator revenue-share reforms:** YouTube's 55% remains the gold standard; direction positive, terms still unilaterally set.
- **Self-regulation (Meta Oversight Board):** hears ~20–30 cases/year, can't touch algorithms/ads/data — legitimacy theater that leaves power untouched.
- **Regulation:** EU DSA has the only real teeth (first non-compliance decision: €120M against X, Dec 2025); US KOSA passed the Senate but died in the House — no federal law. **Net assessment: every alternative that preserved the engagement-advertising core produced cosmetic change.**
